# MYTHOLOGY_COMIC_RULES.md

> **Purpose:** A reusable visual, narrative-presentation, and production specification for a family-friendly Indian mythology comic-book series. Apply these rules to **every new book**, regardless of its characters, deity, source, or episode. Each book receives its own approved story/manuscript and artwork references.
>
> **Visual benchmark:** *The Enemy Who Multiplied — Complete Comic Edition* (the approved 30-page PDF). The benchmark defines **presentation**, not the plots, names, theology, weapons, or character designs of future books.
>
> **Priority:** **Story fidelity > religious/iconographic accuracy > legibility > scene continuity > aesthetic polish > speed.** If an attractive design conflicts with the supplied story or references, keep the story and correct the design.

## 1. Non-negotiable book identity

1. Create a **premium illustrated mythology comic**, not a conventional illustrated manuscript, a slideshow, a chat interface, or a collection of captions over pictures.
2. Use the reference book's **deep midnight-navy canvas, warm metallic-gold ornament, cream/parchment reading surfaces, jewel-toned illustration palette, and finely detailed painterly artwork** as the stable series identity.
3. The tone is **devotional, cinematic, dignified, immersive, and readable by families**. Avoid childish clip-art styling, exaggerated cartoon proportions, generic fantasy armor, and commercial-app UI styling.
4. Each page must feel like a **complete printed book page** with unified typography, decoration, panel hierarchy, and visual storytelling.
5. **No plot invention.** Do not add motivations, genealogies, parents, boon-givers, promises, relationships, victories, characters, weapons, or theological claims that the supplied story does not support. Do not invent religious quotations or pass creative dialogue off as scripture.
6. A new book should be recognizable as belonging to the **same series** while its cast, geography, iconography, events, and emotional palette remain true to that book.

## 2. Fixed visual design system

### 2.1 Palette

Use these as **target UI/layout colors** (approximate digital equivalents, not sampled print specifications). Painted scenes may contain richer shades.

| Role | Target color | Hex | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midnight navy | Deep blue-black | `#071F35` | Exterior page, masthead, footer, panel gutters |
| Antique gold | Warm luminous gold | `#D8AC4E` | Frames, filigree, dividers, ornaments, title accents |
| Bright gold | Pale sunlit gold | `#F0CF76` | Fine highlights, key ornamental emphasis |
| Parchment | Cream ivory | `#F6E8C6` | Numbered narration/caption plaques |
| Speech white | Soft warm white | `#FFF9EF` | Dialogue and thought balloons |
| Ink | Near black | `#251C18` | Main readable text |
| Panel-number red | Deep burgundy | `#692019` | Number badges with gold border and cream numeral |

Accent palette *inside paintings*: saffron, vermilion, crimson, deep emerald, turquoise, peacock blue, royal purple, ivory, bronze, and mountain-sky blue. Gold and navy must remain the **layout anchors** even when scene colors change.

Do not switch an entire page to a random background color, use neon gradients, or give different chapters unrelated design systems. If a scene is night-time, stormy, oceanic, or cosmic, its painting may reflect that while the **frame remains navy/gold**.

### 2.2 Ornamentation

- Use a **full-page thin-to-medium gold ornamental frame** over a navy ground, with lotus-inspired corner medallions, delicate curls/filigree, and fine double rules.
- Gold border details must look **crafted, restrained, and consistent**: decorative richness without covering faces, action, dialogue, or readable space.
- Place narrow navy/gold gutters between panels. All panels align visually; use intentional variations, not random gaps.
- Add understated chapter-divider flourishes, panel borders, and footer cartouches. Avoid oversized ornamental boxes that compete with the actual story.
- Sidebar mottos and footer aphorisms are **optional** and must never invent a moral or doctrine or repeat the paragraph the reader just read. If the story does not support one, omit it.

### 2.3 Typography

- Header/title: elegant **classical serif**, bold or semibold; cream or pale gold, high contrast against navy.
- Series masthead: uppercase serif, widely tracked: `[SERIES / IMPRINT]` (e.g., `KHATAKSHETRA / KATHAS` if the project uses that imprint).
- Main story text: highly legible book serif, sentence case, near-black on parchment/white; never fancy script for long passages.
- Chapter title: large enough to anchor the page but subordinate to the illustrations; scene captions: smaller; dialogue: readable at intended page size; footer: small but not microscopically small.
- Minimize ALL CAPS outside short mastheads, labels, sound effects, and ritual terms that genuinely need it.
- Use typographic quotation marks, correct spelling, clear punctuation, sensible line breaks, and consistent transliteration/names.
- **Prefer typesetting text as a separate editable layer after illustration generation.** Image models can misspell or modify words inside artwork. If text is generated as part of a raster illustration, verify **every word and attribution** against the approved script.

## 3. Page canvas and assembly

1. **Default finished page shape: portrait 3:4.** The approved reference has **1086 × 1448 px** full-page images, assembled into a **30-page PDF with one image per page**.
2. Use 1086 × 1448 px for digital continuity when matching this edition. For true print use, retain **the 3:4 shape** but export at the physical printer's required pixel density, bleed, CMYK/profile, and safe margins; do not assume the 1086 px image is sufficient for every print size.
3. Treat each page as a single **full-bleed, edge-to-edge artwork canvas**. Do not add a white printer-page surround, shrink multiple comic pages onto one PDF page, or introduce arbitrary margins.
4. Never stretch a scene, deform characters, distort panels, or trim critical text when matching dimensions. Reframe/crop thoughtfully or add a new comic page if needed.
5. Include front cover, all numbered story pages, and the correct reflection/source note or back matter, **in explicit order**.
6. Export PDF as **one full comic image per PDF page** with uniform size and orientation. No preview collages, title slides, or audit pages mixed into the reader edition.

## 4. Page architecture

### 4.1 Standard interior structure

- **Top masthead:** fixed series identity in gold on navy, thin gold flourishes.
- **Chapter/page identifier:** sequence number and short, meaningful scene title. Titles may describe a theme; they need not restate the first narration line.
- **Book subtitle/series line:** smaller uppercase serif, e.g. the current book title.
- **Comic panels:** typically **2–4 numbered scenes** per page, with flexible proportions and deliberate reading path.
- **Bottom footer:** navy/gold ornamental rule, centered page number, optional short motifs consistent with that book.

**Not a rigid four-panel template.** Use 1–2 cinematic panels for important revelations, divine appearances, dramatic battles, or emotional peaks; 3–4 panels for conversations and sequential action. Additional inset reaction panels are allowed when they materially help comprehension. Variation is essential to a living comic.

### 4.2 Compositional rhythm

- Mix **full-width horizontal scenes**, **two-column panel grids**, **hero portrait panels**, and **smaller intimate insets**.
- Establish the setting in a wider view, then move closer for faces and exchange, then widen again for consequence where suitable.
- Reserve genuinely large uninterrupted art for important mythic events; **do not shrink a major illustration merely to fit text**.
- Keep left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading order obvious. Do not make balloons cross frame boundaries or force backtracking.
- Panels should have clear action and **visual change** from the previous panel: a new speaker, reaction, movement, revelation, consequence, or meaningful shift in focus.
- Avoid two panels in sequence showing almost the same pose and containing almost the same information.

## 5. The four text channels — never confuse them

### A. Numbered narration plaque

**Purpose:** scene setup, location/time shift, passage of time, actions not naturally spoken, causal transition, or essential context.

**Styling:** short cream rectangular/parchment plaque along the upper edge of the panel; small **burgundy square number** at the left with gold trim and a cream numeral.

**Length guideline:** ideally **one sentence, about 8–22 words**; sometimes two brief sentences when indispensable. Avoid full paragraphs, summaries of the entire panel, philosophical lectures, or repeating dialogue.

### B. Spoken dialogue balloon

**Purpose:** what a named character actually says, using the approved dialogue or a carefully faithful dramatization when adaptation is authorized.

**Styling:** soft white/ivory rounded or oval balloon, subtle dark outline, a clear tapered **tail anchored to the speaker**, placed within that speaker's panel. Dialogue remains the reader's primary channel for conversations.

**Length guideline:** most balloons **5–35 words**; split longer speeches into sequential panels or short balloons rather than crowding a single image. Long religious explanations may instead need another page.

### C. Thought balloon

**Purpose:** a character's inward worry, doubt, memory, or reflection when the manuscript supports that interior perspective.

**Styling:** rounded cloud/soft bubble or restrained inset with **small trailing thought dots** pointing to the thinker; distinct from a spoken dialogue tail. Never present inward thoughts as words another character can hear.

**Use sparingly:** not every page needs one. Thought text must be grounded in the character's actual inner concern, not speculative psychology invented by the agent.

### D. Expository inset / source note

**Purpose:** an explanation too important to omit but too artificial for dialogue, a name/identity distinction, an adaptation note, or a verified reference.

**Styling:** smaller cream side plaque or appendix/back-matter panel with serif text and optional light gold motif; use only when truly needed. It is **not** a fourth duplicate of the story beat.

### Critical anti-repetition rule

**A caption and balloon in the same panel must each contribute different information.** Test this before art generation and again after rendering.

- **BAD:** Caption: “The messenger warned the Goddess to surrender.” Balloon: “I warn you: surrender!”
- **GOOD:** Caption: “His formal proposal gave way to a threat.” Balloon: “Come willingly, or my king will use force.”
- **BAD:** Caption: “Suratha asked why he still cared.” Balloon: “Why do I still care?”
- **GOOD:** Caption: “Even in exile, the king's thoughts returned home.” Thought: “Who is caring for my people?”

The goal is **complementary storytelling, not deleting essential facts**. If removing duplication loses nuance, move that nuance to another panel or an inset—not back into a redundant caption.

## 6. Story fidelity and adaptation

1. **Canonical input:** use only the new book's supplied story/manuscript plus sources explicitly authorized for that book. The reference comic is a **style reference only**, not a source of mythological facts.
2. Preserve: events, cause/effect, order, principal relationships, important quotations, theological distinctions, sacred names, the nature of divine manifestations, and the source's ending.
3. Use **brief, character-natural dialogue** to dramatize the source when necessary, but never create the impression that literary dialogue is a verbatim quotation from scripture.
4. Narrator/character separation matters. A narrator may report a future event; a character must not mysteriously know that event unless the story supports it.
5. Where accounts differ across traditions, **follow the supplied tradition**; do not silently merge variants. If source ambiguity is significant, raise it for review rather than improvising.
6. Do not convert every line of prose into a speech balloon. Some context belongs in narration; some truth is conveyed best by action or reaction with no text.
7. If a scene is information-heavy, **expand the page count**. Never compress the story into unreadable paragraphs or dense tiny lettering.
8. Preserve important spiritual meaning without preaching or adding invented morals. Reflections on modern life, if requested, must be explicitly labeled as editorial reflections, not scriptural quotations.

## 7. Mythological character and iconography consistency

Before illustration, create a **book-specific visual character bible**. Each character record should include:

- Canonical name and approved aliases (including spellings/transliteration)
- Role, relationships, and where they appear
- Age/appearance cues **only if supported or approved**, facial features, hair, complexion, clothing, jewelry, and silhouette
- Divine emblems, number of arms if known, held weapons, vehicle/mount, hand gestures, crown/halo, and associated visual symbols
- Approved reference image(s), plus what must remain fixed vs what can change by scene
- Emotional/physical range, e.g., seated in teaching scenes versus active in battle

Rules:

- **Same character = stable identifying features across pages.** Changes must reflect a story event or explicitly documented artistic convention, not model drift.
- No arbitrary additional/missing hands, fused fingers, floating limbs, duplicated weapons, shifted arm attachments, swapped attributes, missing mounts, or species changes.
- **Multiple arms are acceptable only when appropriate to the authorized iconographic depiction.** A two-armed conversation depiction and a multi-armed emblematic depiction may coexist if the book's art direction explicitly allows them; never write dialogue claiming a transformation happened merely because the drawing differs.
- Respect distinctions among related goddesses/manifestations. Do not swap labels or portray one deity as another through lazy visual reuse.
- When a reference source says “do not change illustrations,” reuse provided scene artwork via faithful cropping/placement; do not claim AI regeneration preserves an image pixel-for-pixel.
- **Hand and face QA** is mandatory for important figures, but do not sacrifice a strong image with needless whole-scene regeneration; fix locally or flag for human review if a correction would alter the story.

## 8. Environmental and cinematic continuity

- Establish a consistent **scene bible** for places: forest/hermitage, palace/court, mountain, river, village, temple, heaven, battlefield, etc.
- Maintain architectural language, flora, landscape, light, weather, and props within the same place and sequence.
- Use setting to signal transitions: an open mountain vista, then an intimate close-up, then a court scene, then a return to the storyteller's forest frame.
- Render sacred stories with visual respect, rich environments, and emotional specificity. Avoid random fantasy monsters, technology, costumes, or scenery unsupported by the text.
- Sound effects (e.g., clash, roar, thunder) may be stylized and sparse; they must match a real event in the story and never obscure lettering.
- Depict conflicts in a manner consistent with the source and intended readership; focus on narrative clarity, not gratuitous visual shock.

## 9. Required production workflow

### Phase 1 — Intake and grounding

- Accept the new story, target language/readership, series imprint, source/tradition, and any approved artwork or characters.
- Identify book title, chapter/scene order, key story beats, theological distinctions, and any ambiguity that needs source clarification.
- Produce a **locked story-beat list** and list of terms that must not change.

### Phase 2 — Character and scene bibles

- Produce **book-specific** character sheets and recurring location references.
- Approve or inherit series framing, type hierarchy, and color palette from this rules document.

### Phase 3 — Script → storyboard

For every comic page, create a structured record:

```yaml
page_number: 12
source_span: "Chapter/paragraph references"
page_title: "Short scene title"
layout: "2-column / full-width / cinematic / mixed"
panels:
  - id: 1
    story_beat: "What actually changes in this panel"
    illustration: "Existing art reference or exact scene composition"
    caption: "Context/action that dialogue does not repeat"
    balloons:
      - type: dialogue # dialogue | thought
        speaker: "Character or null for thought owner"
        text: "Approved exact wording"
    inset: null
    continuity_checks: ["Character attire", "Weapons and hand placement"]
source_fidelity_notes: ["All required facts still present"]
```

- Check every scene against its source. Do not invent a filler panel merely to hit a panel quota.
- Check captions versus balloons for duplicate meaning **before generating art**.

### Phase 4 — Art and lettering

- Render **page by page or in small, controlled batches**. Maintain a shared style anchor and book-specific character references for every generation.
- Prefer generating illustrations **without embedded final text**, then place exact approved captions/balloons in a layout pass so the wording stays stable.
- If generating complete painted pages with text, inspect all writing at 100% zoom; reletter incorrect passages rather than silently accepting altered scripture/story lines.
- Keep one versioned final raster per page; do not overwrite approved outputs accidentally.

### Phase 5 — Two independent QA passes

**Narrative QA:** verify no omissions, invented details, changed speaker, mislabeled divine power, inaccurate quote attribution, missing transitions, or duplicate caption/dialogue.

**Visual QA:** verify page ratio; borders and font hierarchy; legibility; balloon tails; character/hand consistency; stable mounts/weapons; correct panel numbers; no cropped text; visually distinct successive scenes.

For each page, explicitly record **PASS / FIX / ESCALATE** and the reason. Failed pages are not silently included as final.

### Phase 6 — PDF finalization

- Freeze one approved final image for each intended page ID (cover, `01...NN`, reflection/credits).
- Sort by explicit **story order**, not by filename alphabetically or file modification time.
- Check count and **expected chapter/page labels**; missing numbers or double-used numbers are blockers.
- Check pixel-size consistency and orientation across every included page.
- Detect exact and visually near-identical image duplicates, then **visually review** any flagged pair. Recurring characters/backgrounds are permitted; accidental repeated full pages are not.
- Assemble **one untouched full-page comic image per PDF page**. No new page layouts during PDF export, no whitespace or rescaling, no contact sheet in the reader-facing PDF.
- Render PDF proof sheets and inspect first page, middle pages, last page, and every reported anomaly at actual reading size.
- Deliver both the reader PDF and (if useful) a **separate** page-audit/contact-sheet file. Do not insert internal QA artifacts into the storybook.

## 10. Approval gates

- **Book-level gate:** story, source/tradition, character bible, and special iconography constraints locked.
- **Style gate:** first 1–2 comic pages approved against the series visual benchmark.
- **Content gate:** all scripts pass source-fidelity/anti-repetition checks.
- **Production gate:** full set of artwork/lettering approved.
- **Final gate:** ordered PDF passes completeness, duplicate, dimension, and readable-text tests.

An automated agent may proceed through a gate when its criteria are objectively met. It should request human review for unresolved theological variants, confusing source passages, major character/iconography uncertainty, or repeated inability to produce readable lettering.

## 11. Stop/failure conditions

**Never publish a 'final' book if any of the following remains true:**

- A page is missing, duplicated, out of order, or internally numbered incorrectly.
- A character's name, role, weapon, mount, or divine identity changes without source support.
- A speech bubble is assigned to the wrong character, or a thought bubble reads like audible dialogue.
- A numbered narration plaque restates the speech balloon's core information.
- Rendered text is misspelled, garbled, too small, or cropped; especially sacred names and quoted language.
- A scene is rewritten to fit an illustration instead of the illustration fitting the approved story.
- The PDF adds whitespace, changes page proportions, or combines more than one full comic image per page.
- A claimed scriptural quote or explanation cannot be tied to the supplied source.

## 12. One-page checklist (run on every page)

- [ ] Does this page advance the supplied story without adding unsupported facts?
- [ ] Are all speakers and thoughts correctly attributed?
- [ ] Do captions add something **different** from the dialogue?
- [ ] Does panel order read naturally and show purposeful visual change?
- [ ] Is the navy/gold/parchment series identity intact?
- [ ] Is text readable at normal page size?
- [ ] Are important characters, facial features, hands, weapons, and mounts consistent?
- [ ] Does this page have the correct number/title and fit the 3:4 canvas?
- [ ] Has the final render been checked against its locked panel script?

## 13. Minimal instructions for the future book-producing agent

> Use `MYTHOLOGY_COMIC_RULES.md` as the **non-negotiable series style and workflow contract**. Use the **new story supplied for this book** as the only plot/source-of-truth, along with its explicitly approved references. Build character/scene bibles, draft panel scripts, eliminate caption-versus-dialogue repetition, render a coherent mythology comic in the established navy/gold/cream 3:4 format, check source and visual fidelity, then assemble exactly one approved full-page artwork image per PDF page in order. Never borrow the previous book's facts, cast, or plot merely because it is the style benchmark. Escalate unresolved source and iconography conflicts rather than inventing answers.
