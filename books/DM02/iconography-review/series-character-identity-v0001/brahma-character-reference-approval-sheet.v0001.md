## Why this proposal exists

The current DM02 images usually render Brahma as a single-faced, white-haired elderly man in plain drapery. At readable scale he is not reliably distinguishable from a generic sage. The current character bible intentionally left head count, arm count, crown, complexion, garments, and ornaments unspecified. This sheet therefore separates the locked DM02 narrative floor from documented visual traditions and from the proposed series treatment.

## Museum comparison set

- [Brahma, first quarter of the 10th century, Cambodia, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38265): four faces, four arms, multifaceted piled hair, diadems, and beard. Useful for silhouette logic; regional Angkor sculpture, not a DM02 prescription.
- [Vishnu, 10th–11th century, Punjab, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38146): the accompanying Brahma is identified by four heads with three shown. This directly supports the proposed readable three-face view with a fourth implied.
- [Brihmma, early 20th century, West Bengal, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/915368): four-headed, four-armed, crowned, bejeweled, and lotus-seated. Useful as a later Indian printed/painted convention, not proof of the DM02 source text.
- [Vishnu on Ananta, c. 1700, Chamba/Pahari region, Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2018.155): four-headed Brahma on the lotus from Vishnu's navel. Useful for the Anantashayana relationship and painted-page legibility.
- [Anantasayan, c. 1890, Kolkata, Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2003.140): dark Vishnu, multihooded Ananta, navel lotus, and four-headed Brahma. Useful as a later Bengal comparison; its exact hood count is not adopted.

These examples establish recurring conventions and regional variation. They do not constitute theological approval of this proposal.

## Proposed fixed DM02 identity

If a human reviewer approves this sheet, every newly corrected or regenerated DM02 Brahma should use the following identity lock:

1. **Face system:** four faces total. In the default three-quarter camera, three faces are readable—front, left, and right—and the fourth is implied by the rear crown/hair geometry. A strict profile may show two faces plus rear structure. A readable full or medium figure must never collapse to one generic elderly face.
2. **Age and expression:** mature divine creator, composed and alert; white beard and moustache repeated coherently on each visible face. Avoid caricature, frailty, or a generic hermit expression.
3. **Head treatment:** one shared low gold diadem/crown integrated across the multiple heads, with gathered white hair. No turban, random crown swaps, or page-to-page changes in hair length.
4. **Arms:** four arms total whenever anatomy is visible. The front pair performs the event action—prayer, appeal, or gathered attention. The rear pair carries a small rosary and a restrained water vessel as tradition-based identifiers. These objects are visual conventions only and must not become plot actions or source claims.
5. **Complexion and clothing:** warm copper-gold complexion; ivory lower garment; crimson upper drape with restrained saffron accents; consistent gold earrings, armlets, bracelets, and waist ornaments. The same palette and cut persist across Pages 5–13.
6. **Lotus relation:** saturated pink-red lotus with a continuous stem visibly emerging from Vishnu's navel whenever both figures and their origin relation are in frame. Brahma remains lotus-seated or lotus-supported; no invented throne, island, temple, or hamsa.
7. **Scale rule:** if the panel is too small to resolve the faces honestly, crop closer, simplify the environment, or keep the rear faces as clear silhouette masses. Do not solve legibility by reverting to a single-faced sage.

## Explicit exclusions

- No hamsa, Veda manuscript, kusa grass, sacrificial implement, additional weapon, halo, temple, or throne is authorized by this sheet.
