Page 11 alone uses an apparent four-armed Vishnu, while most other full figures use two. A single series model is required before further correction or production.

## Museum comparison set

- [Vishnu Battles Madhu and Kaitabha, c. 1760, Pahari kingdoms, Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1974.46): a four-armed Vishnu just awakened on a multiheaded serpent in this same narrative cycle. The most directly relevant painted comparison.
- [Vishnu on Ananta, c. 1700, Chamba/Pahari region, Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2018.155): purple-toned Vishnu in a yellow-orange wrap reclining on a multiheaded serpent, with Brahma on the navel lotus. Demonstrates that complexion, held emblems, and serpent count vary by workshop.
- [Anantasayan, c. 1890, Kolkata, Cleveland Museum of Art](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/2003.140): dark Vishnu reclining beneath a multihooded Ananta, with four-headed Brahma on the navel lotus. Useful for the relationship, not as a fixed numerical template.
- [Vishnu, 10th–11th century, Punjab, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/38146): tall crown, long garland, chakra, conch, mace, and four-arm structure. Useful for recognizability and silhouette.
- [The Art of South and Southeast Asia: A Resource for Educators, The Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Art_of_South_and_Southeast_Asia_A_Resource_for_Educators.pdf): summarizes four arms, tall conical crown, dark blue-grey skin, and recurring attributes as common Vishnu conventions. This is broad educational guidance, not a scene-specific prescription.

The regional and chronological differences are deliberate evidence against treating one museum object as the only valid form.

## Proposed fixed DM02 identity

If a human reviewer approves this sheet, every newly corrected or regenerated DM02 Vishnu should use the following identity lock:

1. **Face:** one stable youthful-adult masculine face with calm almond eyes, straight nose, defined jaw, and consistent proportions. Sleep changes eyelid state; battle changes expression and posture, not facial identity.
2. **Complexion:** one deep luminous divine blue across all lighting conditions. Highlights may warm or cool, but the base must not drift to grey, purple, pale human skin, or another hue.
3. **Hair and crown:** black wavy shoulder-length hair; the same tall gold `kirita-mukuta` in sleep, awakening, dialogue, and battle. No peacock feather, crown removal, crown redesign, or unexplained hair-length change.
4. **Forehead mark:** one small, restrained pale-gold U-shaped Vaishnava mark, identically proportioned on every readable face. This is a tradition-based series proposal and requires explicit human approval because regional forms vary.
5. **Arms:** four arms total in every readable full or medium figure. In sleeping scenes all four rest coherently; in awakening and dialogue they support posture and gesture; in arm-to-arm battle they grapple or balance without duplicating bodies. Occlusion is allowed only when anatomy remains accountable. A close crop may omit arms outside frame but must not contradict the fixed structure.
6. **Garments:** golden-yellow pitambara with a consistent pleat and waist treatment; deep teal-green uttariya; no costume swaps between repose and battle.
7. **Ornaments:** the same tall crown, gold earrings, layered necklace with one central jewel, long flower garland, armlets, bracelets, and waist belt. The jewel is a stable costume marker; this sheet does not claim a named sacred object.
8. **Implements:** the sourced discus appears only where the locked event permits it, particularly Page 21. Conch, mace, lotus, bow, sword, and extra weapons remain withheld from DM02 unless separately source-cleared and human-approved. Empty hands are preferable to importing unrelated attributes.
9. **Navel lotus:** retain a clear navel origin for Brahma's lotus wherever the relation is in frame. Garment folds and ornaments must not obscure or relocate the stem.
10. **Shesha relation:** Shesha is a traditional multihooded serpent support/canopy. DM02 does not impose an exact hood count. Use an overlapping, depth-layered hood fan with some hoods partially occluded or cropped; never make five, seven, or another number a claimed source fact.

## Explicit exclusions

- No peacock feather, arbitrary halo, extra weapon, Garuda, Lakshmi, temple, island, throne room, or new sacred emblem is authorized by this sheet.
- No exact Shesha hood count is fixed.
- No four-arm requirement is falsely attributed to the locked DM02 text; it is the proposed series convention supported by traditional examples.
- No earlier Vishnu pixel—including the Page 14 or Page 21 proof—is promoted to canonical reference.
- Approval of this identity would not clear Page 11's six-locus Yoganidra withdrawal or qualify as theological certification.
