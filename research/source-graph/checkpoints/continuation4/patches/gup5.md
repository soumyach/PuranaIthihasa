## Moon’s mockery, mitigation, tapas and Bhālacandra site

PARTIAL — 61.1–56

Upāsanā 61.1–4 (gup5-event-001 / gup5-e-001). Brahmā recounts going to Kailāsa and seeing Nārada present an extraordinary fruit to Śaṅkara. Gaṇeśa and Kumāra both ask for it. Śiva asks Brahmā which child should receive it; Brahmā recommends the young Kumāra. Śiva gives it to him, angering Gaṇeśa. Brahmā then returns home intending creation.

Upāsanā 61.5–8 (gup5-event-002 / gup5-e-002). Gaṇeśa takes a fierce form, creates an extraordinary obstacle and frightens Brahmā. Seeing this fierce elephant-faced form and the bewildered Brahmā, Moon laughs with his retinue. Angered, Gaṇeśa curses Moon to become unfit to be seen in the three worlds: whoever sees him will become a great sinner. The deity then leaves for his own abode with Gaṇas.

Upāsanā 61.9–17 (gup5-event-003 / gup5-e-003). Moon becomes discoloured, distressed and described by the unresolved cintāmaye hṛde clause, regretting his childish wrongdoing and loss of beautiful, honoured visibility. The gods hear the curse; Fire, Indra and others approach Gaṇeśa. Their hymn identifies him with creation, protection and destruction, transcendent and qualified forms. They seek refuge, explain that Moon’s invisibility distress affects the whole world, and ask favour so Moon, themselves and the world can gain happiness.

Upāsanā 61.18–19 (gup5-event-004 / gup5-e-004). Pleased by the gods’ praise, the four-goal-giving deity, here speaking as Vikaṭa, invites them to choose a great desired boon, promising even what is impossible in the three worlds.

Upāsanā 61.20–27 (gup5-event-005 / gup5-e-005). The gods ask favour for Moon, saying his restoration benefits all. Gaṇeśa first proposes that Moon remain unseen for one year, its half or half of that, or that they request another boon. They prostrate again. Vikaṭa says his words cannot become false, even if cosmic order changes, but he cannot abandon those taking refuge. He narrows the harmful sight to Bhādra bright fourth: seeing Moon knowingly or unknowingly then brings abhiśāpa and great distress. The gods joyfully accept, shower flowers and leave with permission to speak to Moon.

Upāsanā 61.28–36 (gup5-event-006 / gup5-e-006). The gods tell Moon his mockery of the universal teacher endangered all three worlds. They explain their difficult intercession, warn against Bhādrapada-fourth sight and command him to seek Gaṇeśa’s refuge for purified form and fame. Moon follows. Indra instructs the one-syllable sin-removing mantra; Moon practices severe tapas in deep concentration on the SOUTH bank of Gaṅgā. The duration is literally daśadvādaśavarṣāṇi; a definite ten, twelve or twenty-two years is not chosen without support. Gaṇeśa is pleased and appears.

Upāsanā 61.37–44 (gup5-event-007 / gup5-e-008). Gaṇeśa appears four-armed, huge, wearing red garlands/clothes, red sandal and vermilion, illuminating worlds beyond many suns. Moon trembles, steadies himself, infers the deity has come to grant a boon, bows and hymns obstacle removal, the three worldly goals, cosmic functions, Veda/Brahman and intelligence. He asks pardon for an offence caused by ignorance and compassion rather than abandonment of one taking refuge.

Upāsanā 61.45–50 (gup5-event-008 / gup5-e-009). Pleased by praise and prostration, Gaṇeśa grants Moon his former form again. Bhādra bright-fourth sight nevertheless retains abhiśāpa, sin, loss and foolishness. For the dark-fourth vow, Gaṇeśa is to be worshipped at Moon’s rising and Moon is also to be worshipped and deliberately seen; otherwise the vow is fruitless. He orders Moon to remain as a digit on his forehead and gives a monthly-second grant, retaining the printed nabhasyaśca qualifier without inventing a new audience or month rule. Having received the boons, Moon becomes as before.

Upāsanā 61.51–56 (gup5-event-009 / gup5-e-010). Moon installs a Varada image with gods and sages; they name it Bhālacandra. He builds a jeweled golden temple and worships with sixteen services. After worship, the gods and sages grant that this site become famous as a siddhi-field and give every siddhi to observance practitioners. They return to their places; the deity withdraws his body, and the joyful Moon, regaining his radiance, returns to his own abode.

## Sulabha, Sumudrā and Madhusūdana: curses and inadvertent rescue

COMPLETE — 62.1–44

Upāsanā 62.1–10 (gup5-event-010 / gup5-e-012). Kṛtavīrya’s father accepts the explanation of the dark-fourth meal at moonrise and asks why dūrvā is especially dear to Gaṇeśa. Brahmā introduces Sulabha of southern Jāmbā city, a wealthy, generous and self-controlled kṣatriya versed in Vedas/śāstras and devoted to Vikaṭa. Wife Sumudrā is beautiful, faithful, god/Brahmin/guest-devoted and follows her husband’s mind. Purified after bathing, they sit together to hear Purāṇa. Poor Brahmin Madhusūdana, continually contemplating the supreme deity, arrives for alms in worn clothes; Sulabha joyfully bows.

Upāsanā 62.11–19 (15 printed55) (gup5-event-011 / gup5-e-013). Deluded, Sulabha suddenly laughs at the poor Brahmin. Angered, Madhusūdana curses him to become a suffering plough-pulling bull. Hearing her husband cursed, Sumudrā angrily curses the Brahmin to the printed cakrivān/feces-eating state; he curses her to become a poor, cruel caṇḍālī eating excrement and urine. Abandoning their rare human bodies, Sulabha becomes the endlessly labouring bull, Madhusūdana a donkey, and Sumudrā the violent, impoverished caṇḍālī. The subsequent actual donkey form is recorded without inventing another interpretation of cakrivān.

Upāsanā 62.20–29 (gup5-event-012 / gup5-e-014). The wasted Sumudrā/caṇḍālī wanders south of the city and sees Gaṇeśa’s temple with trees, birds and observance practitioners seeking wishes, sons, liberation or wealth. On Bhādra fourth, a city-wide Gaṇeśa festival meets a torrential storm. Driven and beaten from houses, she enters the temple holding fire; some beat her there, but yogins restrain them. She lights grass to warm herself. Wind carries one dūrvā sprout onto Gaṇeśa’s head. The cold-fearing donkey enters, and the bull, released from the plough, also comes by providence.

Upāsanā 62.30–36 (gup5-event-013 / gup5-e-015). The bull and donkey eat her grass and fight with hooves and horns near the deity while people sleep. Dūrvā falls from their mouths onto Gaṇeśa’s trunk and feet, pleasing him. She strikes them with a staff and eats the offerings. People awaken at hoof sounds, beat and expel her with staff, fist, elbow and stones. Fearing contact, they bathe the deity with enchanted tīrtha water and worship afresh. They also beat all three and close the temple doors, leaving the trio without escape.

Upāsanā 62.37–44 (gup5-event-014 / gup5-e-016). The cries and movements of the trapped trio move Vikaṭa. He considers that their presence caused renewed worship, their dūrvā reached him and they performed many circumambulations. He declares one dūrvā offering on Bhādra bright fourth and circumambulation make a person honoured and worthy of his regard. He decides to take the trio to his own abode and sends Gaṇeśa-shaped Gaṇas with a festive vimāna, Gandharvas and Apsarases. The Gaṇas give them divine bodies, place them aboard and swiftly bring them to Gaṇeśa’s abode before astonished onlookers.

## Analāsura origin, swallowing, cooling attempts and Vighnahara site

PARTIAL — 63.16–51;64.1–31

Upāsanā 63.16–22 (gup5-event-018 / gup5-e-020). Kauṇḍinya recounts a festival in Dharma’s city with gods, Gandharvas, Apsarases, Siddhas, Cāraṇas, Nāgas, sages, Yakṣas and Rākṣasas. Dancing Tilottamā’s covering falls; Yama sees her breasts and desires to embrace and kiss her. Ashamed, he leaves the assembly; his semen falls on the ground. From it arises a flame-garlanded, distorted-faced, cruel man with terrible sounds, burning earth and reaching the sky with his hair, terrifying the three worlds.

Upāsanā 63.23–31 (gup5-event-019 / gup5-e-021). The assembly goes to Viṣṇu, hymns him and seeks help for the world. Viṣṇu goes with them to Gaṇeśa, recognized as capable of destroying the threat. Gods and sages praise obstacle-making/removing, universal witness, world-origin/protection/destruction and wish-granting qualities, asking what other refuge could remove fear. They lament an untimely dissolution and ask why the deity neglects them when all face death.

Upāsanā 63.32–42 (gup5-event-020 / gup5-e-022). Gaṇeśa compassionately appears as a beautiful, strongly radiant child with two arms reaching the knees, fine limbs, ornaments and costly clothes. The gods/sages rise, acclaim and bow, ask his identity and purpose, and infer Brahman’s child-form has come to rescue those who abandoned rites through Analāsura fear. The smiling child confirms their understanding and says he voluntarily came to kill the harmful demon.

Upāsanā 63.43–51 (gup5-event-021 / gup5-e-023). The child announces a means of killing the demon and directs the gods to carry it out. The following strategy clause is preserved as sarvair bhavadbhis taṃ dṛṣṭvā nodanīyo balād aham; its exact intended action remains unresolved. He tells them to watch his and the demon’s remarkable encounter. They joyfully yet uncertainly discuss his capacity and bow. Analāsura approaches as dissolution-fire, burning ten directions and devouring humans. The people cry out and sages flee, warning the child to escape before the fire kills him, comparing it with a great predator. Gaṇeśa stands firm like Himavat; they retreat far away, leaving him there.

Upāsanā 64.1–8 (gup5-event-022 / gup5-e-024). Aśrayā asks Kauṇḍinya what occurred between the steadfast child and the fire-demon. Through Nārada’s narration to Indra, Kauṇḍinya says the advancing demon shakes earth and mountains, roars like thunder, drops birds from branches, uproots trees and leaves the sea without water. The child seizes the fiery demon by māyā power and swallows him before everyone, like the jar-born sage swallowing the ocean.

Upāsanā 64.9–16 (gup5-event-023 / gup5-e-025). Gaṇeśa considers that the demon entering his belly could burn the three worlds seen inside. Indra gives Moon to calm the fire; gods and sages praise Bhālacandra, but the throat-fire persists. Brahmā gives his mentally created maidens Siddhi and Buddhi, described through cooling lotus/water/Moon imagery, and tells him to embrace them. The embrace calms the fire only slightly. Viṣṇu gives a tender lotus, bringing Padmapāṇi praise; Varuṇa pours cold water; Śiva gives a thousand-hooded serpent binding the belly, giving the name Vyālabaddhodara. The throat is still not cooled.

Upāsanā 64.17–23 (gup5-event-024 / gup5-e-026). Eighty-eight thousand sages arrive, each placing twenty-one nectar-like dūrvā sprouts on the deity’s head. The fire finally subsides and Gaṇeśa is pleased. Knowing this, gods and sages offer more dūrvā. He declares even great or small devoted worship fruitless without dūrvā, and none gains its worship-fruit without it. Devotional offering of one or twenty-one at dawn gives a fruit not attained even by hundreds of sacrifices, gifts, vow accumulations, severe tapas or rules earned over crores of births.

Upāsanā 64.24–31 (gup5-event-025 / gup5-e-027). The gods worship again with dūrvā. The joyful deity roars, grants many unspecified boons and withdraws his child form. They call him Kālānalapraśamana, install an image in a built temple and name it Vighnahara. Victory gives the city the name Vijaya; obstacles being removed explains Vighnahartṛ. Bathing, giving, tapas and observance there become inexhaustible through his favour. Kauṇḍinya closes the account to Aśrayā, promises all-sin removal by reading/hearing it, then introduces another old history.

## Janaka’s feeding test and the poor couple’s devotional gift

COMPLETE — 65.1–45;66.1–25

Upāsanā 65.1–6 (gup5-event-026 / gup5-e-029). Kauṇḍinya recounts Nārada visiting Gaṇeśa after many days. The sage bows, praises the fruitful sight and stands with joined hands. Gaṇeśa holds his hand and seats him. Nārada says he came to report an astonishing matter and would depart after bowing. Gaṇeśa asks him to describe everything before returning to his hermitage.

Upāsanā 65.7–16 (gup5-event-027 / gup5-e-030). Nārada reports Janaka of Mithilā as proud, generous and learned in Vedas and Vedāṅgas: he continually feeds and honours Brahmins with ornaments, clothing and gifts, gives wealth to poor, blind and destitute people, and grants petitions. Nārada wonders whether Gaṇeśa’s satisfaction accounts for undiminished wealth. On visiting, Janaka mocks him through pride in Brahman knowledge. Nārada praises Gaṇeśa as giver of the king’s devotional desires, but Janaka claims to be lord, giver, enjoyer, protector, maker, cause and instrument, with none other in three worlds. Angered, Nārada says God alone makes the world, challenges the king’s apparent religious display, and promises evidence soon.

Upāsanā 65.17–28 (gup5-event-028 / gup5-e-031). Having heard the report, Gaṇeśa honours Nārada with arghya, ornaments, flowers and sandal. With permission Nārada goes to Viṣṇu in Vaikuṇṭha. Gaṇeśa comes to Mithilā to test the king’s devotion, wearing a repulsive old Brahmin form with bleeding wounds, flies and missing teeth. He stumbles, faints and falls amid children; people cover their noses and spit. At the royal gate he asks to be announced as an old, hungry Brahmin guest desiring a meal. Janaka orders his admission, observes his filthy clothes, trembling, bleeding and exhaustion, suspects a divine test, and resolves to satisfy him if merit allows.

Upāsanā 65.29–35 (gup5-event-029 / gup5-e-032). The guest says he came after hearing Janaka’s fame and asks food until fully satisfied, promising the merit of one hundred sacrifices. Janaka takes him inside, honours him and serves tasty food. The guest consumes the entire portion in one mouthful, then a mountain-sized portion in one mouthful, then prepared food sufficient for ten thousand. Rice is put to cook in innumerable vessels and he eats every finished batch.

Upāsanā 65.36–45 (gup5-event-030 / gup5-e-033). Some advisers conjecture that the guest is a Rākṣasa and gifts to such beings give no merit; others say even the three worlds could not satisfy him and urge offering grain. All palace, city and village grain is gathered and consumed, still without satisfaction. Messengers report none remains; Janaka lowers his head. The guest departs house to house asking food. Residents explain their grain was collected by the king and already eaten by him. He questions how he can leave unsatisfied after hearing Janaka’s unparalleled giving fame. Amid silence he finds the Brahmin home of Virocanā and Triśiras, enters like its owner, and sees no equipment or metal vessels.

Upāsanā 66.1–9 (gup5-event-031 / gup5-e-034). The couple sits on bare earth, with sky as covering, avoiding contact with metals. They eat unasked food, use water for their rites and maintain purity. Amid flies and mosquitoes their Gaṇeśa image is worshipped with flowers and leaves in exclusive devotion. The disguised guest says Janaka did not satisfy him and ostentatious action cannot preserve sattva; he asks anything in their house that could satisfy him. The couple wonders how they, poor people, can feed one whom an emperor could not, comparing a drop with rivers unable to fill the ocean. He says even a small devotional gift greatly satisfies him; abundant gifts without devotion or given for display are fruitless.

Upāsanā 66.10–16 (gup5-event-032 / gup5-e-035). The couple swears nothing remains except one dūrvā sprout left from the morning Gaṇeśa worship. At the guest’s request Virocanā offers that single sprout with devotion, imagining rice, pāyasa, varied cooked dishes, accompaniments and lickable or suckable foods upon it. He joyfully eats it; his stomach-fire immediately subsides, he gains complete satisfaction and embraces Triśiras.

Upāsanā 66.17–22 (gup5-event-033 / gup5-e-036). Gaṇeśa abandons the repulsive disguise and appears four-armed, lotus-eyed and trunk-bearing, holding lotus, axe, rosary and tusk, wearing costly crown, earrings, divine clothing and scents. He invites the couple to choose their desired boon. They ask for firm devotion wherever they are born, or liberation from the difficult ocean of transmigration, wanting nothing else. He assents with tathā, joyfully embraces Triśiras again and vanishes.

Upāsanā 66.23–25 (gup5-event-034 / gup5-e-037). Kauṇḍinya tells Aśrayā that this is why he offers dūrvā: the deity who remained unsatisfied by innumerable foods gained full satisfaction through one sprout. He states that hearing the offering’s greatness gives all wishes; devoted hearing or telling brings sons, wealth and enjoyment here and hereafter, Gaṇeśa devotion, and liberation for a desireless person.

## Aśrayā’s equal-weight trial and the couple’s abode

PARTIAL — 66.26–31;67.1–40

Upāsanā 66.26–31 (gup5-event-035 / gup5-e-038). The rescuing Gaṇas say Aśrayā still doubts. Knowing her heart, Kauṇḍinya instructs her to take one dūrvā to Indra and convey his blessing and request: obtain gold equal in weight to this sprout, neither less nor more, and bring it back. She accepts the instruction and takes the sprout to Indra.

Upāsanā 67.1–11 (gup5-event-036 / gup5-e-039). Aśrayā asks Indra for gold at her husband’s instruction. Indra says a message could have sufficed for him to send gold; she insists on exactly the sprout’s weight, neither less nor more. He sends a divine messenger with her to Kubera, requesting the exact measure. Kubera laughs at the sage, Indra and Aśrayā for seeking so little, and gives much gold. She refuses it from fear of her husband and doubt about unequal weight. A goldsmith’s scale and then a merchant’s scale fail to balance the dūrvā.

Upāsanā 67.12–21 (gup5-event-037 / gup5-e-040). An oilman’s scale also fails. A pot is attached and gold placed on one side, while the dūrvā’s pan descends. Kubera adds more gold, then all treasury wealth like a mountain; it still does not equal the sprout. He asks his unnamed devoted wife to climb into the pot and proposes to follow if that fails. She enters; it remains unequal. He adds the entire city and himself. Hearing from the messenger, Indra comes on an elephant and enters with his wealth, yet the sprout does not rise. Troubled, he invokes Viṣṇu and Śiva, who arrive with their cities and mount the pot; still the sprout does not rise.

Upāsanā 67.22–31 (gup5-event-038 / gup5-e-041). Śiva, Viṣṇu and Kubera descend. With Varuṇa, Indra, Fire, Maruts, gods, divine sages, Siddhas, Vidyādharas and Uragas, they go to Kauṇḍinya at day’s end. They bow and say his sight removes sin and earlier merit promises future good; his wife has taken all their strength. They confess they do not know the sprout’s greatness: even three worlds did not equal one offered with devotion upon Gaṇeśa’s head; sacrifices, gifts, vows, tapas and study do not match it, nor can they fully know the sage’s exclusive devotion, japa and tapas. All worship Gaṇeśa and the sage with their wives, hymn, dance and sing. The hymn names Brahmā, Hari, Śiva, Indra, Maruts, Fire, Vivasvān, Yama, Śeṣa, Varuṇa, Moon, Aśvins, the lord of speech, Garuḍa, Yakṣa king and Aṅgiras as unable to fully know the deity. Having pleased him, they obtain the sage’s permission and return home.

Upāsanā 67.32–39 (gup5-event-039 / gup5-e-042). Aśrayā now knows dūrvā’s greatness, trusts her husband’s words, worships Vighneśvara with dūrvā and bows to Kauṇḍinya. She reproaches her own doubt, praises his knowledge and compassion and asks pardon while taking refuge. The couple rises at dawn, collects dūrvā, bathes and offers it with exclusive devotion. They worship morning and evening continuously, relinquishing sacrifice, vow and gifts. Knowing this, Gaṇeśa compassionately brings them to his own abode.

## The rescued-trio yogins and the nested dūrvā explanations

PARTIAL — 62.45–49;63.1–15;67.40–59

Upāsanā 62.45–49 (gup5-event-015 / gup5-e-017). Onlookers conjecture that former merit caused the destination. Yogins leave meditation and ask the Gaṇas how these grave wrongdoers gained a rare blessed end despite no visible piety. They would abandon their own observance to do that method: after uncounted dispassionate, air-fed austerity they still have not seen the deity and ask when his abode will be theirs.

Upāsanā 63.1–7 (gup5-event-016 / gup5-e-018). The rescuing Gaṇas tell the yogins that even Śeṣa and four-faced Brahmā cannot fully describe Gaṇeśa, whom Brahmā and Śiva praise. Dūrvā’s greatness is not known even to sages/gods and cannot be attained by sacrifices, gifts, tapas, vows or homa. They introduce an earlier Indra–Nārada conversation: Indra honours the visiting sage, seats him and asks why dūrvā is especially dear to Gaṇeśa.

Upāsanā 63.8–15 (gup5-event-017 / gup5-e-019). Nārada describes the sage Kauṇḍinya in Sthāvara city, with a wooded hermitage south of the settlement and lotus lakes populated by birds and other creatures. He meditates and performs intense tapas before a happy, boon-giving four-armed Gaṇeśa image covered in dūrvā, japping the six-letter mantra. Wife Aśrayā asks why he offers loads of mere grass daily when nobody is ordinarily pleased with grass, and what merit it gives.

Upāsanā 67.40–46 (gup5-event-040 / gup5-e-043). The Gaṇas state no Śeṣa, Brahmā, Hari or Īśvara can exhaust dūrvā’s greatness. Verse40 says the three worlds became equal by its leaf on a scale, while the preceding trial and26 say they did not equal the sprout; these formulations are retained without reconciliation. Remembering dūrvā removes threefold sin because it also recalls Gaṇeśa. Hearing, recounting or meditating the Cintāmaṇi-site greatness brings enjoyment and liberation. They explain that this caused the rescue vimāna for the three: dūrvā fell from the bull/donkey mouths and the woman’s warming grass through strong wind onto Gaṇeśa. Pleased, he purified them and gave his own presence. Even its scent satisfies him, incidentally or with devotion, still more offering it upon his head.

Upāsanā 67.47–56 (gup5-event-041 / gup5-e-045). Brahmā says the king and sages hear the messenger’s unprecedented dūrvā account. After bathing, they and servants worship Vināyaka with sprouts. They obtain radiant divine bodies, hear divine music, board a fine vimāna in divine clothes and scents, and reach Vaināyaka’s abode, some bearing his form. Townspeople who come to the celebration each offer twenty-one dūrvā, enjoy their allotted pleasures and eventually reach Gaṇeśa’s place; the vimāna moves upward through this merit. The discourse instructs devotees to worship with dūrvā, condemns deliberate omission through caṇḍāla and hell language, says seeing a dūrvā worshipper purifies another sinner, and permits one sprout when many are unavailable, multiplying worship a crore-fold.

Upāsanā 67.57–59 (gup5-event-042 / gup5-e-046). Brahmā closes the dūrvā history to the king, calling hearing it sin-destroying, forbidding its telling to someone of wicked understanding and prescribing communication to a beloved son. Indra says the father hears this excellent account from Brahmā, rejoices, bows and returns to his own place with permission and wonder.

## Dream book, family-guru instruction and concise royal outcomes

PARTIAL — 68.1–35;70.26;71.18–29

Upāsanā 68.1–12 (gup5-event-043 / gup5-e-047). Śūrasena asks Indra what Kṛtavīrya’s father did next. Indra says the son-seeking king anxiously considers childlessness after hearing the Sankaṣṭa and dūrvā accounts. Verse3 calls the dreamer Kārtavīrya, while the waking king is explicitly Kṛtavīrya in13; the name variation is retained. He dreams of his father. They embrace, unable to speak through emotion. The father takes his hand, seats him and says Nārada reported his efforts for a son, prompting the father’s visit to Brahmā. Brahmā prescribed Sankaṣṭa-caturthī and promised offspring to the royal son once the vow removes sin. The father says he recorded the instructions, gives the book, and tells him to practice for a year so Siddhivināyaka will be pleased and a son will result.

Upāsanā 68.13–21 (gup5-event-044 / gup5-e-048). The father vanishes; the king, explicitly named Kṛtavīrya, awakens and sees the book in his hand. Recalling the dream, he weeps from sorrow at separation and joy at obtaining the book. His ministers gather, urge care and ask the cause, saying his grief affects them. He explains the father’s appearance, gift, immediate disappearance and instruction to practice for a son, and says wonder, joy and sorrow arise because the book remains in his hand after waking.

Upāsanā 68.22–32 (gup5-event-045 / gup5-e-049). The ministers infer that Gaṇeśa, father of all, came as the king’s father and gave the remedy, arguing the book and true dream otherwise lack an explanation. Indra says the king steadies himself, summons scholars and friends, and asks the book’s meaning. The Brahmins identify a Brahmā–Kṛtavīrya dialogue, Sankaṣṭa-caturthī, detailed moonrise worship, Aṅgāraka-fourth glory, arghya mantras for tithi, Gaṇeśa and Moon, feeding and honouring twenty-one Brahmins with gifts, and distinct ordinary/white-dūrvā benefits. They call it fortunate and beneficial, removing distress by hearing and remembering. King and public rejoice and he honours them.

Upāsanā 68.33–35 (gup5-event-046 / gup5-e-050). Kṛtavīrya gives the scholars abundant clothing, ornaments, jewels, wealth and grain. He summons Atri, explicitly guru of his own family. At an auspicious time he properly honours Atri and Vināyaka and receives the one-syllable vidyā. Controlling senses, meditating Gaṇeśa and japping with exclusive devotion, he actually practices the Sankaṣṭa vow to please Gaṇanātha and obtain a son.

Upāsanā 70.26 (gup5-event-058 / gup5-e-063). Sūta says the king hears and properly performs the vow for relief from all distress. Its power enables him to conquer enemies and enjoy an untroubled kingdom with sons. This closing statement reports the royal outcome concisely; the witness proceeds to a further completion procedure and fuller sonbirth account, so it is not treated as exhaustive closure of the larger Kṛtavīrya family history.

Upāsanā 71.18–25 (gup5-event-061 / gup5-e-066). Kṛtavīrya’s father says Brahmā gave him these instructions for public benefit and tells his son to practice carefully for offspring. Indra says the intelligent performer follows every paternal instruction; verse19 names him Kārtavīrya, retaining the same name issue flagged in68. Learned scholars explain the vow. A gold image with Siddhi and Buddhi is installed in a great pavilion amid Brahmin recitation, Purāṇa learning, singing, dance, heavenly-reaching instrument sounds and some debate with mediators. The king japs, offers homa, worships and feeds many Brahmins. Adorned himself, he gives ten thousand cows to adorned Brahmins, food and many gifts to poor, blind and destitute people, then receives the truthful ascetic Brahmins’ blessings for a son.

Upāsanā 71.26–29 (gup5-event-062 / gup5-e-067). Through the Brahmins’ blessings the royal wife becomes pregnant within a short time and gives birth at an auspicious moment to a son with marks. Delighted, the king gives many gifts, later performs the son’s vratabandha and marriage, and consecrates the knowledgeable son in the realm. Enjoying a good son and pleasures, he reaches the Lord’s state at the end. All officiants, scholars and onlookers reach Gaṇeśa’s abode through his merit. This is the chapter’s concise outcome; chapter72 begins a fuller account of that son and is not yet extracted.

## Prescribed Sankaṣṭa worship and its completion protocol

COMPLETE — 69.1–62;71.1–17

Upāsanā 69.1–9 (gup5-event-047 / gup5-e-052). Śūrasena asks Indra how Brahmā taught Kṛtavīrya the successful Sankaṣṭa vow. Indra recounts the father approaching Brahmā in Satyaloka and asking what people in distress, anxiety, separation from friends, difficult undertakings, or seeking wealth, sons and good fortune should do. Brahmā prescribes the wish-accomplishing vow: daytime bathing with herbs and white sesame, proper intention and Gaṇeśa meditation, and devotional worship with Āgama mantras on dark fourth at night’s moonrise. The king asks the exact worship method.

Upāsanā 69.10–17 (gup5-event-048 / gup5-e-053). After daily rites at moonrise, prepare a clean cow-dung-coated place with a pavilion and honour Gaṇeśa’s seat with kuṅkuma and rice. Set a kalaśa with five gems and a gold vessel; silver, copper or bamboo is permitted when unavailable, and cloth according to ability. Draw the Āgama-prescribed yantra and install a well-formed gold image. Meditate one-tusked, huge, molten-gold-coloured, large-bellied, wide-eyed Gaṇeśa with fire-like eyes, riding a mouse, fanned by Gaṇas, with Śeṣa as sacred thread. Invoke him to remove distress and remain present until the vow ends.

Upāsanā 69.17–37 (gup5-event-053 / gup5-e-058). The parenthetical ritual alternatives recite Puruṣa as thousand-headed, thousand-eyed and thousand-footed, embracing what has been and will be; Virāj arises, and Puruṣa arises from Virāj. Gods perform sacrifice with Puruṣa as offering, spring as ghee, summer as fuel and autumn as offering-material; gods, Sādhyas and sages worship the primordial sacrificial Puruṣa. From the sacrifice arise the curd-ghee mixture and aerial, forest and village animals, Ṛc/Sāman/metres/Yajus, horses, creatures with teeth on both sides, cattle, goats and sheep. The bodily social formula assigns mouth to Brahmins, arms to Rājanyas, thighs to Vaiśyas and feet to Śūdras. The cosmic formula gives Moon from mind, Sun from eye, wind/breath from ear, Fire from mouth, interspace from navel, heaven from head, earth from feet and directions from ears. The gods bind Puruṣa as sacrificial animal with seven enclosures and twenty-one fuel-sticks; the closing alternative praises ancient Sādhyas in heaven. These are prescribed Vedic recitations, without a new narrated Gaṇeśa birth or universal biological identity assertion.

Upāsanā 69.18–29 (gup5-event-049 / gup5-e-054). The prescribed offerings proceed through seat, foot-water, gem-and-fruit arghya, sipping water from Gaṅgā and other tīrthas, pañcāmṛta of milk/curd/ghee/sugar/honey, bathing with Narmadā, Candrabhāgā and Gaṅgā-confluence waters, two garments, sacred thread, sandal, ghee/kuṅkuma rice, campaka/mallikā/dūrvā and other flowers, and fragrant incense. The formulas repeatedly ask acceptance and distress-removal, addressing Gaṇeśa as Gaṇādhīśa, Umāputra, Modakapriya, Lambodara, Ibhavaktra, Gaṇādhyakṣa, Vināyaka, Paraśudhārin, Īśaputra, Mūṣakavāhana, Mahākāya and Dhūmraketu. Parenthetical Puruṣasūkta alternatives accompany many services.

Upāsanā 69.30–39 (gup5-event-050 / gup5-e-055). Continue with lamp, modaka/apūpa/laḍḍū/pāyasa with sugar and ghee-cooked food, coconut/grapes/mango/pomegranate, areca/cardamom/clove/betel leaves, gold dakṣiṇā, and twenty-one dūrvā with a twenty-one-name list. Offer camphor light and campaka/aśoka/bakula/pārijāta flower handfuls. The hymn attributes creation, protection, destruction and universal appearance to the elephant-faced deity; the bowing formula praises obstacle-removal, devotee relief, liberation, knowledge and Vedic foundation. Parenthetical Vedic alternatives include Puruṣa-body social and cosmic emanations.

Upāsanā 69.40–50 (gup5-event-051 / gup5-e-056). After the hymn bow repeatedly and make twenty-one circumambulations according to ability. The prayer says those seeking tasks without worship of Gaṇeśa are ruined. Offer vāyana to the Brahmin teacher and special arghya with fruit, flowers, rice, water and dakṣiṇā. Invoke Heramba for distress-removal and honour Indra and the surrounding world guardians. Prepare ghee-cooked modakas with cooked mung and sesame and other foods/betel as able, then offer twenty-one dūrvā separately using the verse names. This second sequence includes Abhayaprada, Amareśvara, Gajavakra and Sarvavidyāpradāyaka alongside names shared with the earlier list; the distinct printed forms are retained.

Upāsanā 69.51–62 (gup5-event-052 / gup5-e-057). Request satisfaction with the properly directed worship, fulfillment of inner wishes, removal of obstacles and present enemies, destruction of enemies’ intelligence and prosperity of friends. Bow repeatedly and perform one hundred eight homa offerings. For vow-completion prepare vāyana of modakas or laḍḍū/vaṭaka and other foods with twenty-one fruits, wrap in red cloth and give to the teacher. Hear the sacred story and offer arghya to the tithi, then Gaṇeśa, then Moon seven times. The Moon formula calls him milk-ocean-born, arising in Atri’s lineage, accompanied by Rohiṇī. Ask the deity’s pardon, feed Brahmins, eat their offered remainder silently in seven mouthfuls according to ability and comfort, and perform this procedure in all four months.

Upāsanā 71.1–9 (gup5-event-059 / gup5-e-064). The king asks Brahmā to explain udyāpana for public benefit. Brahmā prescribes vow-completion in the first, fifth or seventh month. Make a flower pavilion decorated with various fabrics and a multicoloured sarvatobhadra arrangement; worship on a kalaśa as before with sandal and flowers. Give coconut arghya to the tithi, Gaṇeśa and Moon. Their formulas ask distress-removal; the Moon formula again gives milk-ocean and Atri-lineage origin and Rohiṇī accompaniment. Offer edible, chewable, lickable, drinkable and suckable foods and many fruits.

Upāsanā 71.10–17 (gup5-event-060 / gup5-e-065). Choose a teacher and twenty-one officiants. Perform ten thousand homa offerings with Gaṇānāṃ tvā, or use the root mantra for one thousand, half that, or one hundred eight offerings; then give bali, full offering and vasordhārā. Finish homa and feed Brahmins. Give paired garments, kalaśas, dakṣiṇā and seats according to capacity without deceit about means. Honour the fed teacher with clothes/ornaments and give fruit-vāyana, a winnowing basket filled with pāyasa wrapped in red cloth, the gold Gaṇeśa image with dakṣiṇā, an āḍhaka of sesame, and an adorned kapilā cow with calf. Request the Brahmins’ pardon, praying Vighneśa be pleased. Completing udyāpana gives horse-sacrifice merit.

## Brahmā’s brief earlier-practitioner reports and transmission rules

COMPLETE — 70.1–25

Upāsanā 70.1–5 (gup5-event-054 / gup5-e-059). The king asks who first practiced and disclosed the vow and what merit/results it gives. Brahmā says Pārvatī practiced at Śiva’s instruction for four months when Skanda was gone and saw Kārttikeya in the fifth. Agastya desiring to drink the ocean practiced and drank it through Vighneśa’s favour in three months. Damayantī searching for Nala practiced for six months and then saw him. These are brief source reports; no unprovided journeys or battle details are added.

Upāsanā 70.5–12 (gup5-event-055 / gup5-e-060). Citralekhā had taken Pradyumna’s son away. Grieving and uncertain where or by whom he was taken, Pradyumna hears Rukmiṇī address him as her son. She recalls Śambara abducting him when he was six days old, many years of grief and comparisons with other women’s growing sons, then Lomaśa’s fortunate visit and instruction in Sankaṣṭa-caturthī. She practiced four times; through its favour Pradyumna returned after killing Śambara in battle. She urges him to practice the same vow so he will learn where his son is.

Upāsanā 70.13–15 (gup5-event-056 / gup5-e-061). Brahmā says Pradyumna practices the pleasing vow and hears from Nārada that the son is detained in Bāṇāsura’s city. At Uddhava’s instruction Kṛṣṇa, fearing a battle with Īśvara, properly practices the vow once. He goes to Śoṇitapura, defeats Bāṇāsura in combat, and quickly brings Aniruddha with Uṣā.

Upāsanā 70.16–25 (gup5-event-057 / gup5-e-062). Brahmā says he too performed the vow desiring creation and produced varied creation through its power. Gods, asuras, humans, sages, Dānavas, Yakṣas, Kinnaras, Uragas and Rākṣasas also practiced to quell obstacles. He instructs practice during distress, hearing the story, silent eating with hands between knees while meditating Gaṇeśa, and sharing the Brahmins’ remainder with relatives. Tasks accomplish in few months. Teach neither non-devotees, atheists nor deceitful people, but a son, pupil or virtuous devotee. Calling the king dear, righteous and a worker for public good, Brahmā says to perform this before other vows; men and women should perform it before intended tasks.
